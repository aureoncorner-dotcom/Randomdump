# The Filing Packet

## Eight records requests, ready to send

**Prepared:** 31 July 2026
**Purpose:** generate new evidence rather than rearrange existing evidence
**Clock:** federal FOIA runs 20 business days. Filing the week of 3 August puts first responses around **31 August**, with the realistic bulk landing mid-to-late September.

**Not legal advice.** Statutory section numbers change — California's Public Records Act was recodified in 2023 and the Ethics in Government Act in 2022. Verify current citations before sending. Nothing below depends on a section number being exactly right; the substance carries the request.

## STEP ZERO — Do not file for what is already public

Three of the highest-value documents may already be sitting in the open. Check these first; it costs an hour and can save three requests and six weeks.

| Document | Where | Why it may be free |
|---|---|---|
| **ImmigrationOS Justification & Approval (J&A)** | **SAM.gov** | Under FAR 6.305, agencies must publicly post the J&A for a sole-source award, generally within 14 days of award. **This is the single most important document in Count III and it may already be posted.** Search contract ID 70CTD022FR0000170. |
| Full award record, mods, obligations by agency | **FPDS-NG** and **USAspending** | Award-level data is published by statute. Pull the modification history — the mod chain shows how a $30M award became ~$60M. |
| *Bartz v. Anthropic* full docket | **CourtListener / RECAP** | N.D. Cal. No. 3:24-cv-05417. Free. Final approval order, objections, fee motion. |
| OpenAI–CA AG MOU (executed 27 Oct 2025) | **oag.ca.gov** | Already posted. Read it before requesting compliance records — it defines what "compliance" even means. |
| DHS OIG telemetry report | **oig.dhs.gov** | Reports are public. Only the *management response and corrective action status* needs requesting. |

## Reusable blocks

Paste these into any federal request.

### Block A — Fee waiver

> I request a waiver of all fees under 5 U.S.C. § 552(a)(4)(A)(iii). Disclosure is in the public interest because it is likely to contribute significantly to public understanding of the operations and activities of government, and it is not primarily in my commercial interest. The requested records concern [the expenditure of public funds on / the governance of charitable assets subject to state oversight]. I have no commercial interest in these records. All records I obtain are published in full, without charge, under a CC0 public-domain dedication, and are therefore freely available to any member of the public, journalist, or researcher.
>
> In the alternative, I request classification as a representative of the news media or as an educational/non-commercial scientific requester, and that fees be limited accordingly.

### Block B — Processing conditions

> Please provide records in electronic format, in native file format where available, with metadata intact.
>
> If any portion of a responsive record is withheld, please release all reasonably segregable non-exempt portions, and provide an index identifying each withheld record or portion, the specific exemption claimed, and the justification for each — consistent with *Vaughn v. Rosen*, 484 F.2d 820 (D.C. Cir. 1973).
>
> If you determine that any part of this request is unclear or unduly burdensome, please contact me to narrow it rather than denying it. I am willing to negotiate scope.
>
> If this request is denied in whole or part, please advise me of my administrative appeal rights and the applicable deadline.

### Block C — Expedited processing (use sparingly)

Only where a genuine time peg exists — a pending IPO, a scheduled retreat, an expiring contract.

> I request expedited processing under 5 U.S.C. § 552(a)(6)(E) on the basis of an urgency to inform the public concerning actual or alleged federal government activity. [State the peg: e.g., "OpenAI has filed a registration statement with the SEC; public understanding of the governance conditions imposed by state regulators is time-sensitive to that offering."] I certify that the statements in this request are true and correct to the best of my knowledge and belief.

**Practical note on anonymity.** FOIA and state PRAs require a name and address for a response. If you need distance from your own name, the standard routes are (a) file through **MuckRock**, which submits on your behalf and publishes results — which matches CC0 exactly; (b) file under an organizational identity; or (c) use your real name with a dedicated mailbox and email. Fee-waiver review sometimes requires identifying the requester, so a fully anonymous request plus a fee waiver is the hardest combination.

# REQUEST 1 — California DOJ (Public Records Act)

**Target:** Office of the Attorney General, Public Records Coordinator
**Count served:** I
**Clock:** determination due in 10 days, extendable 14 (Cal. Gov. Code § 7920.000 et seq.)
**Precedent:** this office has already answered a PRA on this subject from the EyesOnOpenAI coalition. The channel is proven open.

> Under the California Public Records Act, I request the following records concerning the Office's oversight of OpenAI, Inc., the OpenAI Foundation, and OpenAI Group PBC, for the period **1 September 2025 to the date of this request**:
>
> 1. All compliance reports, certifications, or attestations submitted to this Office by OpenAI or the OpenAI Foundation pursuant to the Memorandum of Understanding regarding Notice of Conditions of Non-Objection executed on or about 27 October 2025.
> 2. All correspondence between this Office and OpenAI, the OpenAI Foundation, OpenAI Group PBC, or their counsel regarding compliance with that MOU.
> 3. Any appraisal, valuation opinion, fairness opinion, or third-party valuation analysis received or reviewed by this Office concerning the transfer of charitable assets in the 2025 recapitalization.
> 4. Any record identifying whether an independent appraisal of that transfer was requested, required, waived, or received.
> 5. A log or index of records withheld, including date, author, recipient and general subject.
>
> If records responsive to items 1–4 are withheld under the investigatory-records exemption, I request in the alternative: (a) confirmation of the existence or non-existence of any independent appraisal, and (b) any non-exempt transmittal letters, cover correspondence, or calendars reflecting these communications.

**Expect:** partial denial on the open investigation. **File it anyway.** Item 4 is the count — if the AG confirms no independent appraisal was requested or received, Count I's element 3 goes from "we couldn't find one" to "the regulator says there isn't one." That is a different sentence entirely.

# REQUEST 2 — Delaware DOJ (Delaware FOIA)

**Target:** Delaware Department of Justice, FOIA Coordinator
**Count served:** I
**Clock:** 15 business days (29 Del. C. § 10001 et seq.)

> Under the Delaware Freedom of Information Act, I request records concerning this Office's review of the 2025 restructuring of OpenAI, Inc. into the OpenAI Foundation and OpenAI Group PBC, for the period **1 January 2025 to the date of this request**:
>
> 1. All correspondence between this Office and the California Department of Justice concerning the OpenAI restructuring.
> 2. All correspondence between this Office and OpenAI or its counsel concerning the restructuring, the non-objection, or any conditions attached to it.
> 3. Any conditions, undertakings or commitments obtained by this Office in connection with its review, whether or not memorialized in a formal agreement.
> 4. Any valuation, appraisal or fairness analysis received or reviewed.

**Why this one matters more than it looks:** two regulators reviewed the same transaction. If their conditions differ, the gap is the story — it shows what one office asked for and the other did not.

# REQUEST 3 — ICE (FOIA)

**Target:** U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement, FOIA Office
**Count served:** III — this is the load-bearing request
**Clock:** 20 business days
**File only after checking SAM.gov.**

> Under FOIA, 5 U.S.C. § 552, I request the following records concerning contract **70CTD022FR0000170** and the system known as ImmigrationOS:
>
> 1. The complete Justification and Approval document supporting the non-competitive award, including all supporting market research and any determination of the absence of competition.
> 2. The base contract, all modifications, and all Statements of Work or Performance Work Statements.
> 3. All Privacy Impact Assessments, Privacy Threshold Analyses, and System of Records Notices prepared for ImmigrationOS or any system into which it ingests data.
> 4. All records identifying the data sources, systems of records, or interagency agreements from which ImmigrationOS receives or queries data, including any agreement with the Internal Revenue Service, Social Security Administration, Department of State, or any State department of motor vehicles.
> 5. All records concerning accuracy, error rate, false-match rate, testing, validation or audit of ImmigrationOS outputs.
> 6. Any policy governing notice to, or redress for, individuals identified by the system, including United States citizens.

**The scoring items are 5 and 6.** If ICE releases them, you have the error rate. If ICE says no such records exist, you have written confirmation that a targeting system operates with no measured accuracy and no redress route — which is *better*, and it is exactly element 4 of Count III.

Item 4 is the Privacy Act architecture. Interagency agreements are the paper trail of consolidation.

# REQUEST 4 — OMB (FOIA) + the faster ethics route

**Target:** Office of Management and Budget, FOIA Officer
**Count served:** III, element 12 — resolves the Barbaccia question either way
**Clock:** 20 business days

**File the ethics route first — it is faster and not FOIA.** Public financial disclosure reports (OGE Form 278e) and ethics agreements for Senate-confirmed officials are available on request under the Ethics in Government Act, using **OGE Form 201**, submitted to OMB's ethics office or to the Office of Government Ethics. Turnaround is typically days, not months.

Then FOIA for what 278e does not cover:

> I request the following records concerning **Gregory Barbaccia**, Federal Chief Information Officer, for the period **20 January 2025 to the date of this request**:
>
> 1. All ethics agreements, recusal statements, screening arrangements, and written determinations regarding recusal or impartiality concerning Palantir Technologies Inc. or its affiliates.
> 2. All waivers issued under 18 U.S.C. § 208(b) or authorizations under 5 C.F.R. § 2635.502(d).
> 3. All records reflecting his participation in, or screening from, any matter concerning Palantir contracts, including any communication with DHS, ICE, or the Internal Revenue Service.
> 4. Any record documenting the implementation or monitoring of a screening arrangement.

**Charge this carefully.** The House Judiciary letter says it is *unclear whether* he played a role — it does not allege that he did. A clean recusal file closes the question honestly, and closing it honestly is worth as much as opening it. A count you retire yourself is a count nobody else can use against you.

# REQUEST 5 — IRS (FOIA)

**Target:** Internal Revenue Service, FOIA Public Access Portal
**Count served:** III
**Clock:** 20 business days

> I request records concerning contracts between the IRS and Palantir Technologies Inc., including Criminal Investigation's use of the Lead and Case Analytics platform, for the period **1 January 2018 to the date of this request**:
>
> 1. A list of all IRS contracts and task orders with Palantir Technologies Inc., with contract numbers, dates, and obligated amounts.
> 2. All Privacy Impact Assessments and Privacy Threshold Analyses for any Palantir-supported system.
> 3. All interagency agreements, memoranda of understanding, or data-sharing agreements under which taxpayer data or return information is made available to, queried by, or shared with the Department of Homeland Security or U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement.
> 4. All records concerning any application programming interface, data integration layer, or consolidated data environment intended to merge Individual Master File data with other IRS or external data sources.
> 5. All disclosure accountings maintained under 26 U.S.C. § 6103(p)(3) for disclosures to DHS or ICE.

**Item 5 is the sharpest instrument in this packet.** Section 6103 makes return information confidential and requires the IRS to *account for* disclosures. That accounting is a record. Asking for it converts "the IRS improperly shared taxpayer information with ICE" from a court-filing allegation into a countable series.

**Expect heavy redaction under § 6103 itself** — the same statute that requires the accounting protects the contents. Aggregate counts and dates are the realistic yield, and they are enough.

# REQUEST 6 — DHS OIG (FOIA)

**Target:** DHS Office of Inspector General, FOIA Office
**Count served:** III
**Clock:** 20 business days

> Referencing the OIG report "CBP, ICE, and Secret Service Did Not Adhere to Privacy Policies or Develop Sufficient Policies Before Procuring and Using Commercial Telemetry Data," I request:
>
> 1. The full management response from each component to each recommendation.
> 2. All records reflecting the current resolution status of each recommendation, including any closure memoranda and the basis for closure.
> 3. All corrective action plans submitted by CBP, ICE or the Secret Service in response.
> 4. Any subsequent OIG work, ongoing or completed, concerning DHS acquisition or use of commercial data, commercially available information, or contractor-operated data analytics platforms.

**Why:** an OIG report is a finding. A closed recommendation with no corrective action is a *pattern*. Item 4 also tells you whether anyone official is already working your Count III — which is either a shortcut or a warning that you will be scooped.

# REQUEST 7 — USCIS (FOIA)

**Target:** U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services, FOIA Office
**Count served:** III — the both-sides finding
**Clock:** 20 business days

> I request records concerning contracts, task orders, or software licenses between USCIS and Palantir Technologies Inc., for the period **1 January 2020 to the date of this request**:
>
> 1. A list of all such contracts and task orders, with numbers, dates and obligated amounts.
> 2. All Statements of Work.
> 3. All Privacy Impact Assessments and System of Records Notices for any Palantir-supported USCIS system.
> 4. All records concerning data sharing, system interoperability, or common data environments between USCIS systems and ICE systems, including ImmigrationOS or Investigative Case Management.

**This is the least-covered corner of the story.** Everyone has written about ICE. Almost nobody has documented that the same vendor sits on the *benefits* side. ## When they say no

**Constructive denial.** If the statutory deadline passes with no determination, administrative remedies are deemed exhausted for federal FOIA and you may proceed. In practice: file the appeal anyway, in writing, citing the missed deadline.

**Appeal, always.** Appeals are free, take fifteen minutes, and agencies release material on appeal at meaningful rates because a different office reviews it. Never accept a first denial as final.

**Glomar** ("can neither confirm nor deny"). Rare outside national security. If it appears on a *procurement* request, that is itself newsworthy — say so publicly.

**Overbreadth rejection.** Answer with a narrowing offer within 48 hours. An agency that rejects a narrowed request twice has a weaker position on appeal.

**Fee estimate shock.** A five-figure estimate is a soft denial. Respond by narrowing date ranges and custodians, and renew the fee-waiver argument citing your CC0 publication practice.

## Failure modes

1. **Asking questions instead of requesting records.** FOIA compels documents, not answers. Never write "please explain whether."
2. **"All documents relating to."** Guaranteed overbreadth rejection. Every item above has a date range and a defined class.
3. **Filing for what SAM.gov already has.** Check first.
4. **Overclaiming news-media status.** Describe your actual dissemination practice — anonymous CC0 publication is a real and defensible basis. Do not inflate it.
5. **Treating a "no records" response as failure.** It is often the finding. "No records concerning accuracy or error rate" *is* Count III, element 4, in the agency's own voice.
6. **Filing all eight the same day.** Stagger over a week. Simultaneous filing across DHS components can get consolidated and slowed.
