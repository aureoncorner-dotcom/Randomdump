2. Nuclear mass can reach electromagnetic material behavior


The strongest positive result is oxygen-isotope substitution in SrTiO_3.


Replacing


\[
{}^{16}O\rightarrow{}^{18}O
\]


leaves


\[
Z_O=8
\]


unchanged while changing nuclear mass. Yet \(^{18}_3\) from its quantum-paraelectric regime into a ferroelectric state. The original experiment observed a dielectric peak near \(23\) K, electrical hysteresis, and a Raman-active soft mode consistent with isotope-induced ferroelectricity. 


A 2026 first-principles treatment reproduces this isotope-induced ferroelectric transition when quantum and anharmonic phonon effects are included. 


The physically supported chain is therefore


\[
\boxed{
\Delta N
\rightarrow
\Delta M_{\rm nucleus}
\rightarrow
\Delta D_{\rm lattice}
\rightarrow
\Delta(\omega_\nu,e_\nu)
\rightarrow
\text{polar instability}
\rightarrow
P\neq0
}
\]


where \(D_{\rm lattice}\) is the mass-weighted dynamical operator.


This is not neutron magnetic moments creating the ferroelectric field directly. It is a nuclear-mass perturbation propagating through quantum lattice dynamics into an electromagnetic order parameter.


Doped SrTiO_3 supplies the superconducting counterpart: partial ^{18}O substitution increased \(T_c\) by approximately \(1.5\times\), with the sign opposite the elementary conventional isotope effect; the authors connect the unusual response to proximity to ferroelectric instability and polaronic/electron-phonon physics. 


So nuclear mass has a demonstrated route into both


\[
\boxed{P}
\]


and


\[
\boxed{T_c,\ H_{c2}}
\]


through the lattice/electronic system.

3. Response sign is not a property of isotope mass alone

\[
M_{\rm isotope}\rightarrow\operatorname{sgn}\Delta O

Lithium supplies a direct counterexample. Between roughly \(18\) and \(21.5\) GPa, ^{6}Li has higher superconducting \(T_c\) than ^{7}Li and the isotope coefficient is positive and unusually large. Above about \(21.5\) GPa the coefficient reverses sign,


\[
\boxed{\alpha\approx-1.5\pm0.5}.

Pressure/compressed-state context therefore cannot be discarded for that witness.

\boxed{

\[
\boxed{

\pi_{\neg P}(x)=\pi_{\neg P}(y)

\operatorname{sgn}\Delta T_c(x)
\neq
\operatorname{sgn}\Delta T_c(y).

13. The Non-Descent Locus


The chemistry/material audit suggests a geometric object that can be defined independently of any particular material system.


Let


\[
\pi:X\rightarrow Q
\]


be a projection from the retained source state \(X\) to a reduced description \(Q\), and let


\[
W:X\rightarrow Y
\]


be the declared physical witness.


Define the non-descent locus


\[
\boxed{
\mathcal N_W(\pi)
=
\left\{
q\in Q:
\exists\,x,y\in\pi^{-1}(q)
\text{ with }
W(x)\neq W(y)
\right\}.
}
\]


This is the set of quotient states whose fibers contain physically distinguishable source states.


If


\[
\boxed{\mathcal N_W(\pi)=\varnothing},
\]


then \(W\) descends through the quotient and there exists a well-defined reduced witness


\[
\bar W:Q\rightarrow Y
\]


such that


\[
\boxed{
W=\bar W\circ\pi.
}
\]


If instead


\[
\boxed{\mathcal N_W(\pi)\neq\varnothing},
\]


then the projection erased context required by that witness.


The empirical compiler therefore becomes a geometric refinement procedure:


\[
X
\xrightarrow{\pi}
Q
\]


\[
\Downarrow\quad\mathcal N_W(\pi)\neq\varnothing
\]


\[
X
\xrightarrow{\pi'}
Q'
\xrightarrow{r}
Q
\]


where \(Q'\) retains enough additional context to split the heterogeneous fibers.


The goal is not maximal refinement. It is the smallest refinement for which


\[
\boxed{
\mathcal N_W(\pi')=\varnothing
}
\]


on the declared domain and at the declared experimental resolution.


This gives three geometrically distinct regions:


\[
\boxed{
Q_{\rm clean}
=
Q\setminus\mathcal N_W
}
\]


where the quotient is sufficient;


\[
\boxed{
\mathcal N_W
}
\]


where hidden context remains physically relevant; and


\[
\boxed{
\partial\mathcal N_W
}
\]


where a parameter change can cause a previously sufficient reduced description to fail.


That boundary is particularly interesting experimentally. Lithium's pressure-dependent isotope-sign reversal is an example of crossing such a boundary: a reduced isotope/\(T_c\) description that appears adequate in one pressure regime ceases to be adequate in another.


Thus the chemistry lane hands geometry a sharply defined target:


\[
\boxed{
\textbf{Find the shape, connectivity, boundaries, and transport structure of the non-descent locus.}
}
\]


And that gives us a clean Section 13 instead of sticking a decorative number on the document. 😂🔥

14. First Rigorous Geometry of the Non-Descent Locus


Status: formal geometric extension only

14.1 Standing domain and the no-shape lemma


All statements below are relative to a declared source domain \(D\subseteq X\), witness \(W\), and projection \(\pi\). Replace \(X\) by \(D\) and \(Q\) by \(\pi(D)\). Thus \(\pi:X\to Q\) is surjective. This removes empty fibers and makes any descended witness unique on all of \(Q\).


The definition in Section 13 is exact at the level of sets, but it does not by itself determine a geometry.


Proposition 14.1 — No-shape lemma.


For every set \(Q\) and every subset \(A\subseteq Q\), there exist a source set \(X\), a surjection \(\pi:X\to Q\), and a witness \(W:X\to\{0,1\}\) such that


\[
\mathcal N_W(\pi)=A.
\]


Proof. Take


\[
X=(Q\times\{0\})\cup(A\times\{1\}),
\qquad
\pi(q,i)=q,
\qquad
W(q,i)=i.
\]


Every fiber contains \((q,0)\), and it contains a second witness value precisely when \(q\in A\). Therefore the non-descent locus is exactly \(A\). \(\square\)


Consequently, shape, openness, connectivity, boundary regularity, branching, and transport must be earned by additional topological or smooth hypotheses. None follows from the bare definition alone.


14.2 Fiber-image field and sector space


For \(q\in Q\), write


\[
F_q=\pi^{-1}(q)
\]


and define the fiber-image field


\[
\mathcal A_W(q)=W(F_q)\subseteq Y.
\]


Then


\[
\boxed{
q\in\mathcal N_W(\pi)
\iff
|\mathcal A_W(q)|>1.
}
\]


The geometric object carrying all witness sectors at once is


\[
\boxed{
\mathsf S_W(\pi)
=
\operatorname{im}(\pi,W)
=
\{(q,y)\in Q\times Y:
\exists x\in X,\ \pi(x)=q,\ W(x)=y\}.
}
\]


Let


\[
r:\mathsf S_W(\pi)\to Q,
\qquad
r(q,y)=q,
\]


and


\[
w:\mathsf S_W(\pi)\to Y,
\qquad
w(q,y)=y.
\]


Its fiber is


\[
r^{-1}(q)=\{q\}\times\mathcal A_W(q).
\]


Thus:


\[
Q_{\rm clean}
=
\{q:|r^{-1}(q)|=1\},
\]


while


\[
\mathcal N_W(\pi)
=
\{q:|r^{-1}(q)|>1\}.
\]


Over \(Q_{\rm clean}\), the sector space is the graph of a single reduced witness. Over \(\mathcal N_W\), it is multivalued, multisheeted, or vertically extended. The branch/singular locus should be distinguished from the non-descent locus itself:


\[
\mathcal B_W
=
\{q\in Q:
r\text{ is not locally trivial near }q\}.
\]


A region can lie inside \(\mathcal N_W\) while carrying a constant number of locally trivial sheets. Branching occurs where the sector structure changes, not merely wherever more than one sector exists.


14.3 Exact descent, canonical refinement, and monotonicity


Define the kernel relation of a map \(f\) by


\[
\operatorname{Eq}(f)
=
\{(x,y)\in X\times X:f(x)=f(y)\}.
\]


Theorem 14.2 — Exact descent criterion.


The following are equivalent:


1. \(\mathcal N_W(\pi)=\varnothing\).
2. \(W\) is constant on every fiber \(F_q\).
3. \(\operatorname{Eq}(\pi)\subseteq\operatorname{Eq}(W)\).
4. There exists a unique map \(\bar W:Q\to Y\) such that
   \[
   W=\bar W\circ\pi.
   \]


Proof. Conditions 1–3 restate the absence of a same-fiber pair with distinct witness values. If they hold, define \(\bar W(q)=W(x)\) for any \(x\in F_q\); fiber constancy makes this well defined. Conversely, a factorization forces equal witness values on equal \(\pi\)-fibers. Surjectivity gives uniqueness. \(\square\)


Theorem 14.3 — Canonical witness-resolving refinement.


Define


\[
\pi_W:X\to\mathsf S_W(\pi),
\qquad
\pi_W(x)=(\pi(x),W(x)).
\]


Then


\[
\pi=r\circ\pi_W,
\qquad
W=w\circ\pi_W,
\qquad
\mathcal N_W(\pi_W)=\varnothing.
\]


Moreover, if a surjection \(\rho:X\to R\) resolves both maps,


\[
\pi=a\circ\rho,
\qquad
W=b\circ\rho,
\]


then there is a unique map


\[
h:R\to\mathsf S_W(\pi),
\qquad
h(u)=(a(u),b(u)),
\]


such that


\[
\pi_W=h\circ\rho.
\]


Therefore \(\mathsf S_W(\pi)\) is the coarsest exact refinement that simultaneously retains the quotient state and the witness value.


This is a formal completion, not yet a physical explanation. It can remove witness ambiguity by retaining \(W\) itself, but it does not identify the smallest causal or mechanistic context coordinate. That stronger compression still requires the deletion tests and empirical boundaries frozen above.


Proposition 14.4 — Refinement monotonicity.


Suppose


\[
\pi=r\circ\pi'
\]


for a refinement \(\pi':X\to Q'\). Then


\[
\boxed{
r\bigl(\mathcal N_W(\pi')\bigr)
\subseteq
\mathcal N_W(\pi).
}
\]


Proof. If two states share a \(\pi'\)-fiber and have different witness values, they also share the coarser \(\pi\)-fiber. \(\square\)


A refinement can split or eliminate heterogeneous fibers. It cannot create a witness defect whose image was clean at the coarser level.


14.4 Metric shape and tolerance


Assume \(Y\) is a metric space with distance \(d_Y\). Define the fiber diameter


\[
\boxed{
\delta_W(q)
=
\operatorname{diam}\mathcal A_W(q)
=
\sup_{x,y\in F_q}
d_Y\bigl(W(x),W(y)\bigr).
}
\]


Then


\[
\mathcal N_W(\pi)
=
\{q\in Q:\delta_W(q)>0\}.
\]


For a declared tolerance \(\varepsilon\ge0\), define


\[
\boxed{
\mathcal N_{W,\varepsilon}(\pi)
=
\{q\in Q:\delta_W(q)>\varepsilon\}.
}
\]


This is the resolution-relative non-descent locus. Its complement contains fibers whose witness spread does not exceed the frozen tolerance. Exact descent is the special case \(\varepsilon=0\).


Theorem 14.5 — Openness and boundary theorem.


Let \(X,Q,Y\) be topological spaces, let \(Q\) and \(Y\) be Hausdorff, and suppose \(\pi:X\to Q\) is a continuous, surjective, open map while \(W:X\to Y\) is continuous. Then


\[
\boxed{
\mathcal N_W(\pi)\text{ is open in }Q.
}
\]


If, in addition, \(X\) and \(Q\) are locally compact Hausdorff, \(\pi\) is proper, and \(Y\) is metric, then every fiber is compact, \(\delta_W\) is finite and continuous, and for every \(\varepsilon\ge0\),


\[
\mathcal N_{W,\varepsilon}(\pi)
=
\delta_W^{-1}\bigl((\varepsilon,\infty)\bigr)
\]


is open, with


\[
\boxed{
\partial\mathcal N_{W,\varepsilon}(\pi)
\subseteq
\delta_W^{-1}(\varepsilon).
}
\]


Proof. Form the kernel-pair space


\[
R_\pi=X\times_QX
=
\{(x,y):\pi(x)=\pi(y)\}
\]


and the defect set


\[
D_W
=
\{(x,y)\in R_\pi:W(x)\neq W(y)\}.
\]


Because \(Y\) is Hausdorff, its diagonal is closed, so \(D_W\) is open in \(R_\pi\). The map


\[
p:R_\pi\to Q,
\qquad
p(x,y)=\pi(x),
\]


is open because it is built from \(\pi\) and a pullback of \(\pi\). Hence


\[
\mathcal N_W(\pi)=p(D_W)
\]


is open.


Under properness, \(p\) is also proper. For each \(a\), the set where the pairwise witness distance is \(>a\) has open image, while the set where it is \(\ge a\) has closed image. These two statements give lower and upper semicontinuity of the fiberwise maximum \(\delta_W\), hence continuity. The boundary inclusion follows. \(\square\)


Without these hypotheses, \(\mathcal N_W\) need not be open or closed, and \(\partial\mathcal N_W\) need not be regular. The no-shape lemma remains the controlling warning.


14.5 Connectivity and multiscale shape


The tolerance loci form a nested filtration:


\[
\varepsilon_2>\varepsilon_1
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\mathcal N_{W,\varepsilon_2}
\subseteq
\mathcal N_{W,\varepsilon_1}.
\]


This gives precise shape targets:


\[
\pi_0\bigl(\mathcal N_{W,\varepsilon}\bigr)
\]


for connected components,


\[
H_k\bigl(\mathcal N_{W,\varepsilon}\bigr)
\]


for holes and higher topology, and the component merge tree across \(\varepsilon\) for the stability of sectors under changing resolution.


If \(Q\) is locally path connected and \(\mathcal N_{W,\varepsilon}\) is open, its connected components are open path components. If the domain, maps, and metric test are semialgebraic, then \(\mathsf S_W\), \(\mathcal N_{W,\varepsilon}\), and their boundaries are semialgebraic. They therefore have finitely many connected components and admit finite stratifications on the declared domain.


Corollary 14.6 — Boundary crossing along a path.


Assume \(\delta_W\) is continuous and let \(\gamma:[0,1]\to Q\) be continuous. If


\[
\delta_W(\gamma(0))\le\varepsilon
\]


and


\[
\delta_W(\gamma(1))>\varepsilon,
\]


then \(\gamma\) meets the level set


\[
\delta_W^{-1}(\varepsilon).
\]


If the path has clean points immediately before its first entry and non-descent points immediately after, that first-entry point lies in


\[
\partial\mathcal N_{W,\varepsilon}.
\]


This is the exact condition required before calling a parameter value a boundary crossing.


14.6 Smooth specialization


Now assume \(X,Q,Y\) are smooth manifolds, \(\pi:X\to Q\) is a smooth surjective submersion, and \(W:X\to Y\) is smooth. The vertical tangent bundle is


\[
V=\ker d\pi\subseteq TX.
\]


Define the vertical witness differential


\[
\boxed{
A_W=dW|_V.
}
\]


Theorem 14.7 — Vertical differential criterion.


If every fiber \(F_q\) is connected, then


\[
\boxed{
q\in\mathcal N_W(\pi)
\iff
\exists x\in F_q
\text{ such that }
A_W(x)\neq0.
}
\]


Equivalently,


\[
\mathcal N_W(\pi)
=
\pi\bigl(\{x\in X:A_W(x)\neq0\}\bigr).
\]


Proof. \(A_W=0\) on \(F_q\) exactly when the restriction \(W|_{F_q}\) has zero differential. A connected manifold is path connected, so this is equivalent to \(W|_{F_q}\) being constant. Apply Theorem 14.2. \(\square\)


For disconnected fibers, \(A_W=0\) detects constancy on each connected component but is not sufficient for descent. The componentwise constants must also agree. Thus disconnected fibers contribute a discrete sector defect that differential tests alone can miss.


If \(\pi\) is also proper, the proper-submersion theorem makes it a locally trivial bundle with compact fibers. The hypotheses of Theorem 14.5 then hold, so \(\delta_W\) is continuous.


The diameter need not be smooth: maximizing pairs can exchange, collide, or cease to be unique. On a smooth stratum where a unique maximizing pair varies smoothly and avoids metric singularities, \(\delta_W\) is smooth. If \(\varepsilon\) is a regular value on that stratum, then


\[
\delta_W^{-1}(\varepsilon)
\]


is a smooth codimension-one boundary there. Sheet collisions or competing maximizers can instead produce corners, cusps, or lower-dimensional strata. Those are geometric possibilities, not empirical findings.


14.7 Transport, branching, and holonomy


At the topological level, let \(\gamma:[0,1]\to Q\) be a path. Define the witness-transport relation


\[
\boxed{
\mathcal T_W(\gamma)
=
\left\{
\bigl(W(\widetilde\gamma(0)),W(\widetilde\gamma(1))\bigr):
\pi\circ\widetilde\gamma=\gamma
\right\}.
}
\]


This relation uses all continuous lifts \(\widetilde\gamma\) that exist. If \(\pi\) has path lifting and \(\gamma\) lies entirely in \(Q_{\rm clean}\), then


\[
\mathcal T_W(\gamma)
=
\{(\bar W(\gamma(0)),\bar W(\gamma(1)))\}.
\]


Transport is therefore lift-independent at the witness level on the clean locus.


In the smooth bundle setting, choose an Ehresmann connection


\[
TX=V\oplus H.
\]


For a path \(\gamma\), horizontal lifting gives a parallel-transport map


\[
P_\gamma:F_{\gamma(0)}\to F_{\gamma(1)}
\]


whenever the chosen connection is complete along that path. For a loop based at \(q\), define the witness-holonomy defect


\[
\boxed{
\mathfrak h_W(\gamma)
=
\sup_{x\in F_q}
d_Y\bigl(W(P_\gamma x),W(x)\bigr).
}
\]


Proposition 14.8.


If


\[
\mathfrak h_W(\gamma)>0,
\]


then


\[
q\in\mathcal N_W(\pi).
\]


Proof. The points \(x\) and \(P_\gamma x\) lie in the same fiber over \(q\) and have different witness values. \(\square\)


The converse need not hold: a heterogeneous fiber may have no selected loop whose holonomy moves between its witness sectors. Holonomy is therefore a detector of transport-sensitive non-descent, not a complete replacement for the fiber test.


If the sector projection


\[
r:\mathsf S_W\to Q
\]


restricts over a region \(U\) to a finite covering, path lifting permutes its sheets. Loops define a monodromy representation


\[
\mu:\pi_1(U,q)\to
\operatorname{Sym}\bigl(r^{-1}(q)\bigr).
\]


A nontrivial \(\mu\) records global twisting of witness sectors even when their local number stays constant. This is the rigorous transport structure corresponding to the earlier branching-and-holonomy target.


14.8 Coarse boundary versus hidden-coordinate transition


Three objects must remain separate:


\[
q\in\mathcal N_W(\pi),
\]


which is a heterogeneous coarse fiber;


\[
q\in\partial\mathcal N_{W,\varepsilon}(\pi),
\]


which is a boundary in the retained quotient space \(Q\); and a transition along an erased coordinate in \(X\) or in a refinement \(Q'\).


Suppose \(Q'\) retains pressure and


\[
r:Q'\to Q
\]


forgets it. A pressure path \(\gamma'(P)\) can cross a witness transition in \(Q'\) while \(r\circ\gamma'\) collapses the entire path to one coarse point \(q\). In that case the observations establish


\[
q\in\mathcal N_{W,\varepsilon}(\pi)
\]


but do not by themselves locate


\[
\partial\mathcal N_{W,\varepsilon}(\pi)
\subseteq Q.
\]


This is the strict reading of the lithium example. If states drawn from the compared pressure regimes match on every retained coordinate under the frozen same-fiber rule, and their isotope responses differ beyond the declared tolerance, they provide a located non-descent counterexample for pressure deletion. The reported response-sign change near the pressure threshold is a transition in the pressure-refined description. Calling it a boundary of the coarse non-descent locus additionally requires a retained-base path whose fiber diameter crosses the tolerance level.


No frozen chemistry status changes here. The distinction only prevents a source-space transition, a refined-space sheet transition, and a quotient-space boundary from being treated as the same geometric object.


14.9 Empirical estimator and first geometry gate


A finite dataset does not reveal the exact set \(\mathcal N_W(\pi)\) without coverage assumptions. The empirical object must be tied to a frozen tuple


\[
(D,W,\pi,d_Y,\varepsilon,\mathcal E),
\]


where \(\mathcal E\) specifies the preregistered same-fiber equivalence or binning rule, measurement uncertainty, and required coverage.


For observations \(i,j\) assigned to the same empirical fiber, let


\[
[L_{ij},U_{ij}]
\]


be an uncertainty interval for


\[
d_Y(W_i,W_j).
\]


A certified empirical non-descent point requires at least one located pair with


\[
L_{ij}>\varepsilon.
\]


This gives the inner, confidence-qualified estimate


\[
\boxed{
\widehat{\mathcal N}^{\,\rm cert}_{W,\varepsilon}
=
\left\{
q:
\exists i,j\in F_q^{\rm obs}
\text{ with }
L_{ij}>\varepsilon
\right\}.
}
\]


Failure to find such a pair is not proof of cleanliness. A tested deletion remains \(\texttt{DESCENT\_CANDIDATE}\) only when the frozen tolerance and coverage rule are satisfied; insufficient coverage remains \(\texttt{UNRESOLVED}\). A located counterexample supports \(\texttt{RETAIN}\). These are the existing Section 9 classifications, now given a geometric readout rather than replaced.


The first admissible geometry run is therefore:


1. Freeze \(D\), \(W\), \(\pi\), the same-fiber rule, \(d_Y\), \(\varepsilon\), and coverage.
2. Construct the observed sector space \(\widehat{\mathsf S}_W\).
3. Compute certified fiber diameters and sector multiplicities without filling unobserved fibers.
4. Estimate components and boundary candidates for the certified locus, with uncertainty labels.
5. Specify a path-lifting rule or connection before estimating transport or holonomy.
6. Report source-space transitions, refined-space branch changes, and quotient-space boundaries separately.
7. Leave global shape, unobserved connectivity, and universal closure unresolved unless the declared coverage test is passed.


14.10 First rigorous disposition


The first geometric treatment establishes:


1. The bare definition permits an arbitrary non-descent locus; regular shape requires regular hypotheses.
2. The sector space \(\mathsf S_W(\pi)=\operatorname{im}(\pi,W)\) is the canonical object encoding clean fibers, multiple sectors, branching, and context attachment.
3. Exact descent is equivalent to fiber constancy and admits a unique reduced witness.
4. The canonical witness-resolving refinement is universal at the set-theoretic level, but it is not automatically a mechanistic compression.
5. Refinement is monotone: finer descriptions can remove coarse defects but cannot project a new defect into a coarse clean point.
6. Under an open quotient, the exact non-descent locus is open. Under open proper metric hypotheses, the fiber diameter is continuous and tolerance boundaries lie on diameter level sets.
7. Under connected smooth fibers, non-descent is detected by a nonzero vertical witness differential somewhere in the fiber.
8. Connectivity is measured by the components and topology of the tolerance filtration, not inferred from isolated examples.
9. Transport becomes a lift relation generally, parallel transport after choosing a connection, and monodromy when witness sectors form a covering.
10. The current chemistry record supplies located examples motivating the object, but it does not yet determine the global shape, connectivity, boundary stratification, or holonomy of \(\mathcal N_W(\pi)\).

15. Arithmetic witnesses: signs, units, and target-relative recovery


These exact constructed examples extend §§13–14. Each declares a retained map and a target witness. They illustrate information loss and recovery; they provide no new empirical measurement or physical mechanism.


15.1 A coordinate can preserve a relation while losing an operation


On the nonsingular elliptic curve E: y² = x³ + 2x + 2 over F₁₃, let P=(2,1), Q=(3,3), and −Q=(3,10). Direct calculation gives P+Q=(12,5) and P−Q=(11,9).


Take D to be affine input pairs whose sum is affine, π(P,Q)=(x(P),x(Q)), and W(P,Q)=x(P+Q). Both (P,Q) and (P,−Q) belong to D and have retained value (2,3), but witness values 12 and 11. Thus W does not descend through π. Retaining the y-coordinates is sufficient to recover signed addition on this domain; no minimality is claimed.


The Semaev condition asks a different question: whether there exist points with the specified x-coordinates, over an algebraic closure, whose signed sum is zero. This existential relation survives passage to coordinates even though the output for specified signed inputs does not. A solution over the algebraic closure need not be a decomposition over a chosen smaller field. Source: Saito et al., Theorem 1.


15.2 Principal ideals forget units needed by some logarithms


In O=Z[√2], the element u=1+√2 has norm −1 and is a unit, so (u)=(1). All ideal valuations therefore agree for 1 and u. Reduce into F₇ by √2↦3. Then 1↦1 and u↦4. Since 3 generates F₇*, their base-3 logarithms modulo 3 are 0 and 1. On the declared two-element domain D={1,u}, this target logarithm does not factor through α↦(α).


There is also an explicit distinguishing local character. The residue ring O/3O is F₉. For α invertible modulo 3, define L(α)=(α⁸−1)/3 mod 3O; the numerator is divisible by 3. Multiplication gives L(αβ)=L(α)+L(β), because the product of the two divisible remainders vanishes after division and reduction. Here u⁸=577+408√2, so L(u)=√2 and L(1)=0. Its √2 coefficient distinguishes the pair.


This character repairs the stated two-element example. It does not prove that one coefficient recovers every target logarithm on a larger domain. General Schirokauer-map applications must establish their own unit-rank, kernel, modulus, and precision conditions. Source: Thomé, NFS course.


15.3 Preserve the declared target subgroup


For a group homomorphism φ:G→H and a subgroup C≤G, the restriction φ|C is injective exactly when ker(φ)∩C={e}. Indeed, φ(c₁)=φ(c₂) exactly when c₁c₂⁻¹ lies in that intersection. Full injectivity on G is unnecessary for information recovery within C.


Applying this observation requires an actual map and subgroup. Weil restriction, a subsequent map to a Jacobian, and a computational speedup are separate assertions. Arithmetic-geometric descent and factorization of a witness through a retained map are distinct uses of the word descent.


15.4 Predictive closure: corrected prime-sequence example


For U(n)=2n+1 on integers n≥2, let r(n) record primality of n and whether n is Sophie Germain. Then r(3)=r(5)=(true,true), but r(U(3))=r(7)=(true,false), while r(U(5))=r(11)=(true,true). The Sophie Germain label supplies one-step prime information without making r autonomous. The full explanation belongs in Working Master §2: GEOMETRY + GQG — Working Master


15.5 Corrections governing reuse of the research packet


The packet is a source of examples and outlines, not a new verified master. Apply these qualifications whenever its arithmetic survey is reused:


Primality: SymPy acceptance alone does not certify the two displayed large integers. Both exceed 2⁶⁴; the documented large-input test is strong BPSW probable primality. Retain a probable-prime or unverified status unless a separate proof or certificate is supplied. Source: SymPy isprime documentation.

The elliptic-net norm identity also retains Sₙ² and explicit additional factors; do not replace it with an unqualified equality to Sₙ. Source: Saito et al., Theorems 1 and 4.

## 19. Geometric recovery, conditioning, and global validity

The gear calculations supply explicit mathematical examples for three distinct questions: whether a witness is determined by the retained data; how errors in those data affect the answer; and whether a local construction remains valid on its whole declared domain. The set-level descent theorem answers the first question. Quantitative regularity and global geometry answer the others. These are additions to the present models, not a claim of new priority in the mathematical literature.

**A measured-profile collision and a usable repair.** On C=[−1,1]², let G_w(x,y;t)=20[(1−w)x²+w x⁴]+10y²+tx, with w∈[0,1], normalized x,y, and gap coefficients in micrometres. For the family parameter w, retain π(w)=(G_w(−1,0;0),G_w(1,0;0))=(20,20). The witness A(w)=area{G_w(x,y;0)≤2} differs: A(0)=0.4442882938 and A(1)=0.8792167529 in normalized projected-area units. Hence A does not descend through these endpoint measurements. At the known tilt t=4, the minimum also differs: x*(0)=−0.1, x*(1)=−0.3684031499.

An admissible interior measurement m=G_w(1/2,0;0)=5−(15/4)w identifies this specified one-parameter family: w=(4/15)(5−m). If its additive error is bounded by ε, the clipped estimate has |ŵ−w|≤4ε/15; the compatible parameter interval has diameter at most min(1,8ε/15). The repair assumes this family, known calibration and a present measurement. It is not a reconstruction theorem for arbitrary surfaces or permission to use a future witness as an input.

**Exact recovery need not be Lipschitz.** For the quartic member and |t|<80, x*(t)=−cuberoot(t/80). It is unique and exactly determined, but |x*(t)−x*(0)|/|t|=80^(−1/3)|t|^(−2/3) diverges. Thus empty non-descent locus does not imply bounded incremental sensitivity. For the quadratic member, x*=−t/40 while the minimum is interior. Total movement from a baseline and the local derivative are different witnesses.

**A sufficient stability condition, including active boundaries.** Let C⊂R^d be nonempty, compact and convex, and f be differentiable on a neighborhood of C and μ-strongly convex on C, μ>0. Put x_ℓ=argmin_C[f(x)+ℓ·x]. The variational inequalities at x_ℓ and x_m, added together, and strong monotonicity of ∇f give μ||x_ℓ−x_m||²≤(m−ℓ)·(x_ℓ−x_m). Therefore ||x_ℓ−x_m||≤||ℓ−m||/μ. Boundary minima are included; replacing this constrained result by −H⁻¹ℓ is valid only when that unconstrained point is feasible. For G_w, μ=min(40(1−w),20) for 0≤w<1 in the declared normalized Euclidean coordinates.

**Uniform surface error controls normalized gap regions.** Let f,g be continuous on the same nonempty compact domain C and ||f−g||∞≤ε. Their minima m_f,m_g obey |m_f−m_g|≤ε, so ||(f−m_f)−(g−m_g)||∞≤2ε. With Ω_f(δ)={x∈C:f(x)−m_f≤δ} and negative-threshold sets empty, Ω_f(δ−2ε)⊆Ω_g(δ)⊆Ω_f(δ+2ε). This is a set enclosure, not equality of areas or a pressure law. If f is also μ-strongly convex on convex C, any minimizer x_g obeys ||x_g−x_f||≤2√(ε/μ): f(x_g)≤f(x_f)+2ε, while constrained strong convexity gives f(x_g)≥f(x_f)+(μ/2)||x_g−x_f||².

**A trace or first jet does not determine a surface.** f(u,v)=u²+v² and g(u,v)=u²+2v² have equal values and equal gradients along v=0, but transverse second derivatives 2 and 4. On R² their unit sublevel areas are π and π/√2. More generally, finitely many sampled finite jets cannot identify an arbitrary smooth surface: a smooth bump supported in an unsampled open region preserves those jets. A declared finite-dimensional model, coverage argument or global bound is needed.

**From samples to a global bound.** On a compact metric domain, if a signed violation function v is L-Lipschitz and sample points form an h-net, then sup_C v≤max_i v(x_i)+Lh, by taking a sample within h of each x. Thus max_i v(x_i)+Lh≤0 certifies v≤0 everywhere. The metric, domain coverage and valid global L are premises, not consequences of seeing a dense-looking grid. Without them, report a finite sample check. Local envelope tangency additionally needs regularity, trimming, finite end caps and a global non-interference argument.

**Units and representation.** If normalized coordinates map to physical millimetres by X=D x, D=diag(a_x,a_y), and gap heights are recorded in micrometres, the physical gap Hessian is H_phys=10^(−3)D^(−T)H D^(−1), in mm^(−1). Projected physical area is a_x a_y times normalized area. At tangent contact in orthonormal physical coordinates the gap Hessian describes relative curvature; an arbitrary chart needs its metric. Under an invertible affine spatial map A, normal covectors transform by A^(−T), followed by normalization. Shape equality without the coordinate map does not establish equal physical curvature.

The present extension adds 20 exact identities, 1,640 constrained tilt-stability cases and 1,230 bounded surface-perturbation cases. The written arguments supply the general conclusions; these finite computations check their specified examples. No chemistry, behavior, sampler replay, physical Q2, or temporal outcome was tested by these calculations.
