# The Conduct Docket

## Tech-Elite Evidence Audit, v2 — conduct lane

**Research date:** 31 July 2026
**Status:** Evidence dossier, version 1 of the conduct lane
**Relationship to v1:** This does not retract the Golden Dawn audit. It replaces its *question*.

---

## Why this document exists

The v1 audit asked a **membership** question: is there a documented initiatory line from the Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn to Sam Altman, Peter Thiel, or the modern technology sector? It answered that question correctly and negatively.

But a membership question about private associations is unfalsifiable by construction. Searching it produces only two outcomes — a leak, or the phrase "not documented." The second outcome reads as exoneration no matter how carefully it is caveated. Every hour spent in that lane converts into a defensive asset for the subjects of the audit.

This document asks a **conduct** question instead: what does the public record establish about how permission, provenance, and refusal are actually allocated?

That question generates records. Contracts. Charters. Sworn testimony. Attorney-general correspondence. Procurement identifiers. Docket numbers.

> The occult hypothesis is the softest available allegation. If it were proven tomorrow it would be legally inert. The documented conduct is worse, and it is admissible.

**Standing rule, retained from the corpus:** No blood guilt. No unified hidden actor. No monster required. Function over person. Convergence remains the null model. Nothing below asserts coordination between the parties named; each file stands or falls on its own instruments.

## Executive finding

The record establishes, at instrument level, a permission architecture with the following properties:

1. **A charitable entity now holds undefined, unappealable authority over an object of general public consequence** — and can retain that authority after divesting its economic stake in it.
2. **Federal data siloed by statute has been consolidated by a single private contractor**, largely through sole-source awards, with the enforcement and benefits sides of the same system served by the same vendor.
3. **Private off-record convenings place officials with contract-oversight authority in the same closed room as executives of firms holding those contracts.**
4. **The courts have priced past extraction while simultaneously foreclosing the creator-standing remedy** — converting a liability into an incumbent moat.
5. **Infrastructure costs are being socialized under contested but non-trivial evidence**, with the dispute itself unresolved and the disclosure regime weak.

None of this requires a covenant. That is the finding.

## Evidence classes (conduct lane)

The v1 letter grades (A–H, X) classified *kinds of connection* for a lineage question. They do not transfer. Conduct requires classes that track **instrument strength**:

| Class | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **P1** | Primary instrument — charter, contract, MOU, articles of incorporation, settlement order, sworn testimony |
| **P2** | Government record — procurement database entry, IG report, congressional letter, AG correspondence, FOIA/PRA return |
| **P3** | Named-source investigative journalism from an outlet with a corrections policy |
| **P4** | Leaked document, independently verified by a named outlet |
| **P5** | Interested-party analysis — advocacy coalition, plaintiff filing, opposing politician. Usable; interest must be flagged |
| **P6** | Contested empirical claim — competent studies disagree |
| **X** | Inference exceeding the record |

**Retained from the corpus:** the invocation/operation split. Invocation is language. Operation is a demonstrated change in money, access, employment, rights, surveillance, governance, or enforcement. Everything in this docket is filed under operation or it is not filed.

**Added for this lane:** a *burden column*. For every mechanism, name who absorbs the cost when it fails. A permission architecture is legible mainly through its incidence.

## FILE 1 — The Class N share

**Docket:** Governance instrument | **Class:** P1

The October 2025 recapitalization split OpenAI into the OpenAI Foundation (501(c)(3)) and OpenAI Group PBC. The Foundation holds roughly 26% of the PBC; Microsoft roughly 27%; employees, former employees and investors the remaining ~47%. The 100x profit cap is gone. The word *safely* no longer appears in the mission.

The instrument that matters is not the equity. It is a share class.

Per the EyesOnOpenAI coalition — a group of 50+ nonprofit, labor and philanthropic organizations, writing in *Fortune*, 22 July 2026 — the Foundation's control derives from **a special share of Class N stock** conferring near-complete authority over board composition *and* sole authority over all "safety and security decisions." Those decisions are **undefined in the articles of incorporation**. Because the Class N share sits alongside the common stock, the Foundation **can divest its entire 26% economic stake and still control the board**.

Read that against Section III of the Necropolis document. This is not an analogy to a priesthood-function. It is one, executed as a security:

- **restricted provenance** — the governing term is undefined in the charter;
- **licensed interpreter** — one board decides what the undefined term means;
- **no appeal** — no external body construes it;
- **decoupled from economic exposure** — authority survives divestment.

**Burden:** in a public listing, retail and pension investors hold the residual risk of a governance term whose content no purchaser can read.

### The valuation gap

When the for-profit arm converted from subsidiary to separate PBC, **no independent appraiser confirmed the Foundation received fair value for the charitable assets it surrendered.** California AG Bonta's non-objection was explicitly conditional on binding commitments under state charitable trust law (MOU executed 27 October 2025). In response to a public-records request, the California AG **confirmed an ongoing investigation into OpenAI**.

### The IPO overlay

OpenAI filed a confidential S-1. **Note a source conflict worth resolving before publication:** filing date is reported variously as 22 May 2026 and 8–9 June 2026 across secondary outlets; treat as unresolved pending the public prospectus. Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley are reported as underwriters. *NYT* reported 25 June 2026 that the board is leaning toward 2027. The $1 trillion figure is a reported target, **not a filed number** — do not repeat it as fact.

Meanwhile the SEC has floated a disclosure regime that would require **less** disclosure at higher valuations. If OpenAI lists, it would be the first 501(c)(3) to take a controlled entity public.

**Falsifier:** publication of articles of incorporation defining "safety and security decisions" with an enumerated scope and an external appeal route; or an independent fairness opinion on the 2025 asset transfer.

## FILE 2 — The conflict portfolio

**Docket:** Fiduciary conduct | **Class:** P1 / P2 / P3

Altman draws roughly $65,000 a year from OpenAI and holds no direct equity in it. Per *Wall Street Journal* reporting, he holds stakes **exceeding $2 billion** in companies that transact with OpenAI or its partners; he and affiliated funds have invested in **400+ companies**, many in sectors OpenAI depends on.

Specific transactions under examination:

| Entity | Reported personal position | Proposed OpenAI action |
|---|---|---|
| Helion Energy | Shareholder since 2014; ≥$375M invested, stake estimated ~$1.7B; board seat | Altman proposed OpenAI commit ~$500M to a ~$1B round |
| Stoke Space | Shareholder via Hydrazine family office | Approached re: OpenAI data-center partnership |
| Merge Labs | Board seat; reportedly no equity | OpenAI announced backing, January |

**The structural point is sharper than "self-dealing."** Because he holds no direct OpenAI equity, his financial interests are *only loosely aligned with OpenAI's own performance* — a point made by the six state AGs, not by critics of AI. The conflict is not that he profits when OpenAI wins. It is that he can profit when it doesn't.

Formal actions on the record:

- **House Oversight Committee** (Chair Comer) letter, May 2026, demanding conflict-of-interest documentation and all records of the audit committee formed after the 2023 ouster; testimony demanded by 22 May.

- **Sworn testimony**, May 2026: Altman stated he has "always been recused." Board chair Bret Taylor testified Altman had been forthright, proactive and transparent. President Greg Brockman testified he holds stakes in Altman's family fund and in startups Altman founded.

**Record both sides.** The recusal claim is on the record and has not been rebutted with a specific document. What has *not* been produced is the audit-committee record that would test it. That gap is the investigable object — not the accusation.

**Burden:** the OpenAI Foundation's charitable beneficiaries, and any future retail purchaser.

**Falsifier:** production of complete recusal minutes for the Helion, Stoke, and Merge transactions.

## FILE 3 — The consolidation contract

**Docket:** State data architecture | **Class:** P2

Palantir federal contract value, per USAspending: **$4.4M (2009) → $541.2M (2024) → $970.5M (2025)**, exceeding $900M in a single year of the current administration.

**ImmigrationOS.** ICE awarded Palantir a **$30M sole-source modification** — Federal Contract ID **70CTD022FR0000170** — justified by reference to Executive Orders 14159 and 13773; subsequently reported at ~$60M. The sole-source justification rests on Palantir having been embedded in ICE's Investigative Case Management since 2014. That is a self-reinforcing lock: prior incumbency is the stated reason no competition is possible.

**Scope.** Reporting and filings describe ingestion spanning IRS, Social Security, passport, DMV and license-plate-reader data, and — per internal documents, court testimony and SEC filings — roughly **80 million Medicaid records**. Palantir now holds contracts with **both the enforcement arm (ICE) and the benefits arm (USCIS)** of the immigration system.

**IRS.** Palantir's Lead and Case Analytics platform has been used by IRS Criminal Investigation since 2018 to aggregate federal datasets; figures reported at **$130M+** (Intercept / American Oversight) and **$180M+ across 26 contracts** (Tax Notes / USASpending). *These are different scopes — reconcile before citing a single number.*

**Why this is the Necropolis thesis with invoice numbers.** The ACLU's analysis is the load-bearing one: consolidation of data that Congress deliberately siloed under the **Privacy Act of 1974** into centralized per-individual dossiers, extending to visa holders and US citizens. The statute exists precisely to prevent this. The mechanism your document theorizes as "the omniscient eye" has a contract number and an appropriations line.

**Oversight on the record:**

- Goldman/Wyden/Velázquez letter to DHS and ICE, 14 April 2026 — 11 documented requests, response deadline 24 April.
- House Judiciary Democrats' immigration-contractor letter, flagging that OMB Federal CIO **Gregory Barbaccia** is a former Palantir employee and stating that **it is unclear whether he played any role** in the sole-source award. *Represent this as an open question, not a finding — the letter itself does not assert influence.*
- POGO investigation into Stephen Miller's reported financial stake in Palantir.
- DHS Inspector General report finding CBP, ICE and Secret Service did not adhere to privacy policies before procuring commercial telemetry data.
- Thirteen former Palantir employees signed a public letter to NPR condemning the ICE work.
- New Jersey congressional delegation and State Investment Council raised concerns as pension-fund trustees holding Palantir stock.

**Company position, recorded:** Palantir states it operates as a *data processor* — customers manage their own data — and denies building a master database or enabling mass surveillance of Americans.

**Burden:** people misidentified by a system with no published error rate, no independent audit, and no pre-deployment congressional debate.

**Falsifier:** publication of ImmigrationOS accuracy metrics, an independent audit, or a competitive re-solicitation.

**Docket:** Access architecture | **Class:** P4

In June 2026 the Swiss researcher **maia arson crimew** found an open directory in the source of dialog.org; WIRED independently verified the contents and obtained the registration list for the 2026 retreat — **222 registrants, 87 first-time**, scheduled 12–16 August at Powerscourt Hotel outside Dublin.

Jim Himes**, six members of the PayPal Mafia, a former Middle East chief of intelligence, a sitting ambassador, and **Gen. **The v1 audit filed this as "no documented magical curriculum."** That is an occult-frame verdict rendered on a governance object, and it buried the finding.

The finding is structural and needs no curriculum:

> Officials with oversight or appropriations authority over surveillance and data contracts convene off the record with executives of firms holding those contracts, under an explicit expectation of non-disclosure.

**"Build-a-Cult" is the least significant item on that list.** It is invocation. The roster is operation. Do not lead with the session titles; they are the part that makes the document dismissible.

## FILE 5 — The price of the corpus, and the moat it built

**Docket:** Provenance and standing | **Class:** P1

This file is the one that most directly tests your Necropolis thesis — and it partly cuts against you. Record it anyway.

**Bartz v. Anthropic** (N.D. Cal., No. 3:24-cv-05417). Judge Alsup's merits ruling split the question: training on **lawfully acquired** books was transformative fair use; **acquiring and storing 7M+ pirated books was not**, and was not cured by later declining to train on them. Statutory damages at up to $150,000 per work put theoretical exposure in the hundreds of billions. The case settled at roughly **$1.5 billion** — reported as the largest copyright settlement in US history — with final approval granted by Judge Martinez-Olguin on approximately **20 July 2026**. Roughly **$3,000 per work across ~500,000 works**; class counsel requested $300M in fees. Terms cover **past training-data use, not outputs**, and include destruction of the pirated dataset.

Related: *Elsevier et al. v. Meta*, S.D.N.Y. No. 1:26-cv-03689, filed 5 May 2026 by five publishers and author Scott Turow, naming Zuckerberg personally on a torrenting theory.

### The three findings that matter for your argument

**1. The remedy you propose was partly foreclosed, in writing.** The court held that authors are **not entitled to licensing revenue for training**, and that a market for training-data licensing is not one the Copyright Act entitles authors to exploit. Section II of the Necropolis document argues that creators require enforceable live standing. A federal court has now said, on the merits, that copyright does not supply it. Your argument therefore has to move to *contract, labor, statute, or state law* — not copyright. Cite the adverse holding. It makes the rest of the document credible.

**2. Provenance became legally load-bearing.** Defendants who can document clean licensing chains are surviving motions and settling better; defendants with piracy-tainted pipelines are not. Discovery now turns on the provenance of individual works. Your Section VIII demand — *provenance must be made inspectable* — is being imposed by litigation rather than by ethics.

**3. The settlement is a moat, not a penalty.** Pricing a previously free input retroactively advantages whoever already consumed it at zero cost. A new entrant must license what incumbents took. This is your "refusal capture" mechanism appearing in the legal record: a safeguard operating as a one-way valve, favoring the parties it nominally sanctions.

**Burden:** the ~500,000 authors receiving ~$3,000 per work, and every future entrant priced out of a corpus the incumbents already hold.

## FILE 6 — Who pays for the furnace

**Docket:** Cost incidence | **Class:** P6 — CONTESTED

**File this one honestly or it will sink the document.**

**Evidence of socialized cost:**
- *Fortune*, 14 July 2026: data centers have already added roughly **$23 billion** to US electricity prices.
- Utilities requested **>$29B in rate increases in H1 2025** — double H1 2024.
- Dozens of utilities received **~700 GW** of data-center interconnection requests in 2025; total US consumption in 2023 was **477 GW**.
- Residential retail prices, March 2021 – March 2026: **+94% DC, +74% MD, +73% ME, +58% NY**.
- PJM has hit record capacity prices three years running.
- 30+ states introduced 300+ data-center bills in 2026; New York ordered the first statewide moratorium in July 2026.

**Evidence against a cost shift:**
- E3's study for Virginia's **JLARC** — the world's largest data-center market — found **no evidence of historical cost shift** to residential or small commercial customers.
- E3's facility-level analysis found Amazon sites averaged ~**$3.4M in net surplus revenue** each; under some conditions large loads *lower* rates.
- **Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory** (2026) attributes rising rates to multiple drivers: equipment costs, aging grid, clean-energy requirements.
- PolitiFact rated the widely circulated **267% figure** as referring to *wholesale* prices, not residential bills. **Do not use that number.**
- At least 38 large-load tariffs have been established since 2018, 30 of them in 2025–26.

**The defensible claim** is narrower than the popular one and still substantial:

> Costs are being incurred at a pace that outstrips the ratemaking process's capacity to allocate them, under a disclosure regime weak enough that the allocation question cannot currently be settled from public data. The March 2026 White House "ratepayer protection pledge" is **non-binding**.

The honest finding is *unresolved allocation under inadequate disclosure*, not proven theft. State that, and the rest of the docket becomes harder to dismiss.

## Where this case is weak

State these before an opponent does.

1. **Energy is contested and you cannot win it on current data.** Lead with disclosure failure, not theft.
2. **Recusal is unrebutted.** Altman's testimony stands unless the audit-committee record contradicts it. Absence of the record is not evidence of the violation.
3. **Palantir's data-processor defense is legally coherent** and has not been rejected by a court.
4. **The Dialog leak came from a hack.** Provenance of the underlying data is contestable, and republishing personal detail on private attendees converts the finding into a liability.
5. **The copyright ruling went against creator standing.** Any document arguing for enforceable creator refusal must now argue *around* Bartz, not from it.
6. **Convergence still explains a great deal.** Restricted provenance, graded access and licensed interpretation recur across bureaucracies, militaries, universities and startups because they solve coordination problems. Nothing here establishes shared agency between Files 1–6.
