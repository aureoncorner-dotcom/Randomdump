## 1. The Material Receipt: 4Q321 Is an Integration Artifact


*Jacobus reconstructs* does more than show that two manuscripts discuss the Moon. It recovers a numerical discontinuity that reveals an intermediate operation.


The continuous lunar model produces an ideal alternating sequence of 29- and 30-day months, with mean lunation 29.5 days. Three 364-day years contain:


```text
3 × 364 = 1,092 days.
```


Thirty-seven ideal lunations contain:


```text
37 × 29.5 = 1,091.5 days.
```


The triennial schedule therefore carries a residual:


```text
ε = 1,092 − 1,091.5 = 0.5 day.
```


If the lunar sequence continues, year four should inherit that residual and shift the checkpoint date. The artifact instead restarts the first-year schedule. The next three-year block is duplicated rather than recalculated from continuous lunar state.


The operative chain is therefore not:


```text
lunar observation → roster dates.
```


It is:


```text
lunar model → three-year lookup table → reset/duplication → six-year priestly roster.
```


That half-day is a compiler fossil. It exposes the seam between the phenomenon and its administrative representation.


The same document recovers a second separation. The X series behaves as calendrical month-start data generated by the month-length table, while dwqh behaves as a distinct astronomical/checkpoint series. The roster artifact joins layers that were calculated for different jobs:


```text
4Q321 = priestly-course index ⋈ month-boundary series ⋈ lunar-checkpoint table.
```


The Timekeeper is therefore not required to be one named official. The historically recovered object is stronger and more durable: a protocol stack connecting specialized desks.


Whether the reset resulted from deliberate design, inherited convention, or mechanical duplication does not change the operational finding. The surviving artifact resets the address table and drops the continuously accumulated residual. Intent is not needed to establish the mechanism.
