And Babylonian time knowledge definitely enters later Jewish literature


The low-hanging receipt is the calendar.


Following conquest and sustained Babylonian/Persian contact, the Babylonian month names were adopted into Jewish calendrical usage: Nisan, Iyyar, Sivan, Tammuz, Av, Elul, Tishri, Marheshvan, Kislev, Tevet, Shevat, Adar. 


Cambridge goes further: conquest changed both Judean civil dating and reckoning of regnal years. 


Ezekiel is another bridge. Current Oxford scholarship describes it as displaying a high level of familiarity with Babylonian culture and ideas, combining Babylonian conceptions with Judean religious ideology in the early-sixth-century exile community.

Follow-up work shows the Enochic division of the day/night cycle into 18 parts can be mapped mathematically onto the Babylonian 360-unit scheme.
