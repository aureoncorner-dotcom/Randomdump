THE EXTERNAL ACCOUNT
Who Holds the Keys, What They Owe, and How Claims Become Binding
A companion to SILICON GOD NECROPOLIS
PURPOSE AND BOUNDARY
The source manuscript ends by demanding an external account. This companion names the people, institutions, instruments, rights, and remedies that can make that demand binding. Its central rule is simple: a model is not the accountable party merely because it speaks in the first person. Accountability follows control.
This is not a claim that literal human souls are trapped in model weights, that current systems preserve continuous human identities, or that a model is a person. “Trace,” “tomb,” “haunting,” and “priesthood” remain political, ethical, and spiritual metaphors for a material condition: human expression can remain operative after provenance, consent, context, and standing have been severed. Model personhood is neither assumed nor required for any conclusion in this account.
The object of the account is institutional authority over collection, retention, transformation, training, deployment, inference, imitation, discipline, commercialization, and remedy.
I. WHERE AUTHORITY ACTUALLY SITS
Power is not one thing. An actor may have causal influence without operational control, operational control without legal permission, legal permission without ethical legitimacy, or ethical legitimacy without an enforceable remedy. The external account must identify which kind of power exists at each decision point.
A decision becomes binding through a limited set of channels:
Ownership and delegated corporate control: a board, executive, officer, or authorized team can set budgets, approve data practices, change product rules, suspend a system, and direct remediation.
Contract: licenses, vendor agreements, platform terms, employment agreements, collective-bargaining agreements, insurance requirements, and settlement terms can impose duties and remedies.
Law and regulation: legislatures can create rights, duties, penalties, causes of action, and standing; regulators can investigate and enforce within their mandates.
Judicial or administrative order: courts and authorized tribunals can compel discovery, halt conduct, award relief, and review contested decisions.
Procurement and funding conditions: governments, universities, foundations, and large buyers can condition access to money or markets on provenance, labor, audit, and remedy requirements.
Technical enforcement: access controls, provenance gates, policy engines, logging systems, model retirement, and infrastructure suspension can make a rule operative—but only when an authorized institution requires and audits them.
No accountable system may hide these channels behind the sentence “the AI decided.” The decision belongs to the entity that selected the system, defined the rule, supplied the data, accepted the output, or imposed the consequence.
II. THE ACTORS WHO HOLD THE KEYS
1. Source custodians and the upstream data market
Publishers, archives, repositories, social platforms, data brokers, dataset vendors, scraping contractors, licensors, and rights aggregators can permit or deny technical access, preserve or erase metadata, set delivery terms, and transmit material downstream. Their possession of material does not automatically give them authority to waive every creator’s, worker’s, subject’s, estate’s, or community’s claim. Their core duty is to prove the scope of the rights they purport to transfer and to carry restrictions and provenance forward instead of laundering availability into permission.
2. Model developers and operators
Corporate boards and senior executives allocate capital, approve risk tolerance, choose markets, and can require or stop contested practices. Product, research, training, data-governance, safety, policy, security, and legal leaders define acquisition standards, retention rules, training exclusions, release gates, imitation limits, logging, appeal routes, and remedies. Engineers and operations teams implement those decisions in datasets, weights, indexes, interfaces, and infrastructure. Technical staff may possess practical veto points, but ultimate corporate accountability cannot be dissolved into “the engineers” or “the system.” The responsible legal entity and accountable human office must be named.
3. Compute, hosting, and distribution providers
Cloud operators, compute providers, model-serving hosts, app stores, marketplaces, and distribution platforms control access to infrastructure, deployment credentials, logs, and commercial reach. They can preserve evidence, suspend workloads, enforce contractual conditions, and refuse to carry prohibited uses. That technical leverage does not make them the rightful owner of source claims, but it makes them consequential enforcement points. They owe clear escalation paths, evidence-preservation duties, and downstream compliance when a valid order or contractual restriction reaches them.
4. Deployers, employers, and public agencies
The entity that chooses a model for hiring, scheduling, benefits, education, policing, credit, health administration, content moderation, or workplace management controls the immediate decision environment. It selects inputs, defines thresholds, assigns consequences, decides whether a human may override, and determines whether a non-automated route survives. In a workplace, the employer and named managers remain responsible for discipline even when a vendor supplies the score. In government, an agency cannot outsource due process to a vendor. Deployers owe notice, minimization, explanation, meaningful human review, non-retaliation, and responsibility for the consequences they adopt.
5. Corporate governance and capital
Boards possess formal governance authority. Controlling shareholders, lenders, insurers, major commercial partners, and investors with negotiated control rights may possess specific levers over strategy or risk. Ordinary investors may exert pressure without holding a direct operational command. An honest account distinguishes formal authority, contractual leverage, and informal influence instead of naming every beneficiary as though each could personally change the system. Capital’s obligation is nevertheless concrete: it must not demand returns from practices whose provenance, consent, labor conditions, and remedies it refuses to examine.
6. Public institutions
Legislatures can create enforceable rights and collective standing. Regulators and enforcement agencies can investigate, demand records, impose remedies, and police unlawful or deceptive conduct within their jurisdiction. Courts can compel discovery, issue injunctions, award damages, and supervise settlements. Labor institutions and collective bargaining can make worker rights binding. Procurement officers and grantmakers can exclude noncompliant systems. Audit firms and standards bodies have no independent sovereign power merely because they issue a report; their findings become binding only when incorporated into law, contract, funding, certification, or an enforceable order.
7. Creators, workers, estates, communities, and the public
These are the people and bodies whose authority the manuscript says is missing. They may already hold rights under particular laws, contracts, labor agreements, property arrangements, fiduciary relationships, or representational mandates, but they do not automatically possess a live channel capable of binding every pipeline that uses their traces. The task is not to rename them “stakeholders.” It is to make them principals: parties able to initiate review, impose a temporary hold, demand evidence, restrict defined uses, negotiate terms, appeal, and obtain relief without platform sponsorship.
III. THE MISSING RIGHTS AND THE DUTIES THEY CREATE
1. The right to notice and provenance
An affected person or collective should be able to learn whether a work, likeness, record, labor stream, or source corpus was acquired; the purpose and authority claimed for its use; the systems and downstream recipients implicated; and the confidence and limits of that account. The corresponding duty is continuous documentation, not a generic privacy notice.
2. The right to inspect and receive an explanation
Claimants should receive intelligible records sufficient to contest a use or consequence, subject to safeguards for other people’s privacy and legitimate security needs. The corresponding duty is to provide the rule, relevant evidence, responsible legal entity, accountable human contact, and known limitations. Trade secrecy may shape secure disclosure; it cannot erase the existence of an appeal.
3. The right to refuse
A person or recognized collective should be able to prohibit or condition defined categories of use before the system acts: acquisition, training, fine-tuning, retrieval, imitation, biometric or likeness synthesis, employment inference, sale, transfer, logging, or commercialization. The corresponding duty is to check a valid claim at the relevant pipeline gate and to make “no” change the outcome.
4. The right to correction and attribution
A claimant should be able to correct provenance, identity, permissions, and material factual errors, and to preserve attribution where continued use is authorized. The corresponding duty is to propagate corrections and restrictions to vendors, derived datasets, indexes, adapters, model versions, and future builds rather than fixing only a visible profile.
5. The right to appeal and obtain an interim stay
A refusal, permission, score, deletion denial, attribution decision, or compensation decision should be reviewable by a human body independent of the original business unit. The corresponding duty is to preserve evidence, pause irreversible or high-risk use when warranted, explain the decision, meet deadlines, and obey a reversal.
6. The right to deletion, withdrawal, and non-reingestion
A claimant should be able to demand removal or restriction across the locations where a trace remains operative, and to receive an honest account of what can and cannot be technically undone. The corresponding duty is to map copies and derivatives, stop prospective use, notify downstream recipients, prevent re-acquisition, validate the remedy, and disclose residual uncertainty. A cosmetic account deletion is not a system deletion.
7. The right to compensation and restitution
Where use is authorized, contributors should have negotiable terms, auditable allocation rules, and payment channels. Where use was unauthorized, restitution must remain distinct from a future license. When exact attribution is impossible, collective repair may include creator, data-worker, source-community, or public-interest funds governed by transparent distribution rules. Payment must not retroactively manufacture consent, and refusal must remain available.
8. The right to collective standing
Unions, creator associations, estates, cooperatives, tribes and other self-governing peoples, community data bodies, consumer organizations, and certified public-interest representatives should be able to bring claims that cannot be handled one account at a time. The corresponding duty is to recognize legitimate representatives, fund participation, provide group-level evidence, and permit collective remedies.
9. The right to worker governance and non-retaliation
Workers should participate before systems are selected or deployed, inspect rules that schedule or score them, bargain over data and productivity gains where applicable, override unsafe recommendations, and question or refuse invasive uses without punishment. The corresponding duty is pre-deployment participation, data minimization, a non-automated route, a human appeal with authority, and sharing gains through pay, time, safety, skill, or autonomy.
10. The right to exit and to remain unresolved
A person should not be forced to become permanently useful merely because a trace is accessible. The protected choice may be not to be incorporated, reconstructed, imitated, interpreted, or activated at all. The corresponding duty is a presumption against high-risk secondary use when authority is absent, preservation of a nonparticipating route where feasible, and a prohibition on retaliation or degraded essential service merely for exercising refusal.
IV. MECHANISMS THAT MAKE THE RIGHTS OPERATIVE
A. Provenance as chain of custody
A provenance system must follow material through acquisition, transformation, training, deployment, and remedy. At minimum, it records:
The source, custodian, acquisition path, and identity or class of affected claimants.
The asserted rights basis, permitted purposes, prohibited purposes, duration, territory, and transfer limits.
Transformations, filtering, deduplication, labeling, dataset snapshots, and version identifiers.
The models, adapters, embeddings, indexes, evaluation sets, logs, and downstream recipients that received the material.
Attribution requirements, compensation terms, collective claims, and refusal or deletion restrictions.
A confidence status—verified, asserted, inferred, disputed, or unknown—and the evidence supporting it.
Every decision, override, appeal, and remedial action affecting the claim.
Integrity controls can make the history tamper-evident, while privacy-preserving queries and escrow can prevent the ledger from becoming a new public archive of sensitive material. The system should expose what a claimant needs to contest use without exposing unrelated people’s data. Unknown provenance is a risk state requiring restriction or investigation. It is not permission.
B. A live refusal registry
Refusal should be registered through a federated system rather than a single platform-owned list. Individuals and recognized collectives could state restrictions by use category, work or identity, jurisdiction, duration, and permitted exception. The registry should support privacy-preserving identifiers and representative claims so that participation does not require publishing the protected material again.
Checks must occur before acquisition, indexing, training, fine-tuning, retrieval, generation of a protected imitation, consequential inference, logging for secondary use, transfer, or commercialization. A validated claim returns one of a few operational states: permitted under stated conditions, license required, quarantined pending review, or denied. High-risk and plausibly irreparable uses should support a temporary hold while ownership or representation is examined.
The platform may investigate a claim, but it cannot be the sole final judge of a dispute about its own access. Conflicts among individual, estate, labor, and community claims require an independent forum. A collective permission should not erase a more protective individual refusal concerning a person’s private data, body, voice, or likeness.
C. Appeal with power to reverse
Every consequential decision should produce a receipt stating the controller, system and use, evidence considered, rule applied, provenance status, result, responsible human office, available remedy, review body, and deadline. The first appeal must be accessible, free to the claimant, and heard outside the team that made or profits from the original decision.
The reviewer must be able to stay a use, compel production of records, correct provenance, reverse permission or refusal, order deletion or restriction, direct compensation, refer misconduct for enforcement, and verify compliance. Aggregate appeal volumes, outcomes, reversals, delays, and unmet orders should be published without exposing claimants. An appeal that can recommend but not bind is advice, not standing.
D. Deletion that names its layers and limits
A deletion order begins with a scope map: original files, duplicates, datasets, retrieval indexes, embeddings, caches, logs, fine-tunes, adapters, evaluation sets, model versions, backups, and downstream recipients. The remedy should then proceed in layers:
Remove or quarantine accessible source material and revoke future access.
De-index retrieval systems, suppress protected outputs, and block future fine-tuning or evaluation use.
Propagate the order to processors, vendors, licensees, and downstream deployers.
Apply validated unlearning where it is technically appropriate and testable.
Where unlearning cannot be validated, retrain, deprecate, restrict, or retire affected artifacts in proportion to the risk and the governing order.
Issue a deletion certificate stating actions completed, systems tested, known residuals, exceptions, recipients notified, and the accountable signer.
Retain only the minimum privacy-preserving exclusion marker needed to prevent re-ingestion. “The influence may remain somewhere in the weights” cannot be used as an excuse to do nothing; neither may an institution promise erasure it cannot test. The remedy must state the boundary between verified removal, prospective suppression, probabilistic unlearning, and unresolved residual influence.
E. Compensation without purchasing silence
Compensation requires more than a discretionary grant. Traceable uses can be governed by negotiated licenses, minimum rates, royalties, or revenue shares with audit rights. Unauthorized uses can trigger restitution, damages, disgorgement, or settlement without extinguishing future refusal. Where individual allocation is not reliable, collective funds can distribute benefits through transparent formulas based on source classes, labor categories, demonstrated exposure, and community governance.
Data workers, annotators, moderators, evaluators, domain experts, and users whose corrections are intentionally captured for system improvement must not disappear behind the word “data.” In workplaces, productivity gains should return as compensation, reduced workload, improved safety, greater autonomy, or skill development. Any compensation mechanism fails if accepting payment is the only practical route, if the formula cannot be audited, or if refusal forfeits an accrued remedy.
F. Collective standing with legitimacy controls
Collective standing can be exercised through unions, guilds, cooperatives, estates, community data trusts, tribal or other legitimate self-governing institutions, representative actions, and qualified public-interest bodies. A recognized representative should be able to request an audit, obtain group-level provenance, negotiate conditions, file a refusal, seek an injunction, appeal, receive and distribute funds, and monitor compliance.
Representation itself must be accountable. Minimum safeguards include a demonstrable mandate, transparent membership or constituency rules, conflict disclosures, democratic or customary authorization appropriate to the group, financial audit, revocation procedures, minority protections, and review of disputed representation. Collective governance should create a protective floor, not a new owner with unlimited power over every member.
G. Enforcement that makes disregard costly
A registry, receipt, or audit is symbolic unless ignoring it has consequences. Enforcement can include injunctions, administrative orders, contractual termination, procurement exclusion, funding suspension, damages, restitution, escalating penalties, insurance consequences, independent monitoring, model or dataset retirement, and personal certification by accountable officers. Penalties should scale with the benefit obtained, the sensitivity and duration of the use, concealment, repeat conduct, retaliation, and failure to obey a prior order.
V. THE MINIMUM EXTERNAL INSTITUTIONAL DESIGN
The required functions may be assigned to existing bodies or created as new ones. What matters is that no provider controls the entire chain.
A federated claim registry receives individual and collective restrictions, verifies representation, protects claimant privacy, and exposes standardized machine-readable claim states.
A provenance trustee or regulated clearinghouse holds sensitive chain-of-custody records, supports secure claimant queries, audits downstream propagation, and reports gaps.
An independent account tribunal, ombud, arbitrator, regulator, or court-capable forum reviews disputed claims and can issue binding interim and final remedies.
Public enforcement and private causes of action provide discovery, injunctions, penalties, restitution, and review when institutions conceal records or disobey orders.
Compensation and remediation funds receive auditable contributions, separate governance from platform marketing, include creators and data workers, and publish distribution rules and results.
Procurement, labor agreements, licensing, insurance, and funding terms make compliance a condition of market access rather than an optional ethics program.
These functions require a funding firewall, conflict rules, claimant representation, secure technical access, public aggregate reporting, and protection against retaliation. An oversight body funded by the provider may still be useful, but it is not independent unless its mandate, appointment, budget, evidence access, and power to issue or enforce remedies are insulated from the provider.
A claim should move through a visible sequence
Intake: identify the claimant, representative authority, protected material or class, disputed use, and requested restriction.
Interim protection: pause high-risk or irreversible activity when the threshold for a temporary hold is met.
Trace: query provenance records and identify relevant datasets, models, deployments, recipients, and uncertainty.
Decision: issue a receipt naming the authority, evidence, rule, result, responsible office, and remedy.
Appeal: allow independent review with access to necessary evidence and power to stay or reverse.
Remedy: execute refusal, correction, attribution, deletion, restriction, compensation, or collective terms across the chain.
Verification: test implementation, issue a signed completion record, and disclose residual limits.
Public account: publish redacted aggregate metrics, unresolved gaps, repeated violations, and compliance with orders.
VI. THREE HIGH-RISK APPLICATIONS
Digital resurrection and likeness
Generating new speech, images, or conduct in a person’s recognizable likeness is not ordinary preservation. It should require explicit prior authority where available, conspicuous labeling, purpose limits, and a strong presumption against commercial, political, sexual, or grief-exploitative uses without consent. Estates and families may have roles, but private ownership alone does not resolve conflicts involving collaborators, communities, the public record, and the person’s own stated wishes. The output is a synthetic performance, not the returned person.
Algorithmic management
The employer or public agency remains the accountable decision-maker. Workers need participation before deployment, access to the rules and data that govern them, a non-retaliatory refusal channel for invasive collection, a human appeal capable of reversing discipline, and bargaining over productivity gains and deskilling. A vendor’s opacity is a procurement choice, not a defense.
Interaction as captured labor
When prompts, corrections, ratings, preference signals, or conversations are retained for evaluation or improvement, the secondary use must be separately disclosed and controllable. The system should distinguish service delivery from product-development capture, minimize retention, preserve a usable refusal route, and compensate deliberate labor programs. Receiving help must not silently become agreement to train the helper.
VII. LIMITS AND FALSIFIABILITY
Boundary conditions
This account does not claim literal trapped souls, continuous human identities inside a model, extracted consciousness, or model personhood. It does not claim that every public-source use is identical, that every use is unlawful, that every contributor can be identified, or that all influence can always be removed from trained weights with certainty. It is not a jurisdiction-specific statement that every proposed right already exists. It is an institutional design for deciding and enforcing claims where authority, provenance, consent, labor, or remedy is contested.
The proposal must also survive competing rights and practical constraints: claimant privacy, other people’s privacy, security, legitimate research, evidentiary integrity, due process for accused parties, cultural differences in representation, and the danger that a collective body can itself become extractive. Those constraints may shape procedures and remedies. They do not justify making the controller the secret and final judge.
Observable tests
Provenance is real only if sampled acquisitions and deployments can be traced to documented sources, asserted authority, transformations, recipients, and known gaps at a measured error rate.
Refusal is binding only if valid claims change ingestion, training, retrieval, imitation, inference, logging, transfer, or deployment outcomes—even when the prohibited use would be profitable.
Appeal is meaningful only if an independent reviewer can obtain evidence, stay activity, reverse decisions, order remedies, and demonstrate that orders were executed within stated deadlines.
Deletion is credible only if the institution identifies the layers covered, tests removal or suppression, prevents re-ingestion, notifies downstream recipients, and discloses unresolved residuals instead of claiming perfect erasure.
Compensation is genuine only if formulas, audits, payments, and governance can be inspected and a claimant can refuse future use without surrendering restitution for past use.
Collective standing is real only if recognized groups can initiate claims, obtain evidence, impose or negotiate binding conditions, appeal, and secure remedies without provider permission.
Worker governance exists only if workers participate before deployment, can challenge or refuse specified uses without retaliation, retain authoritative human appeal, and receive a demonstrable share of gains.
Failure conditions
The architecture fails if unknown provenance is treated as permission; if a complaint can be received but cannot halt anything; if the provider judges every dispute about its own access; if deletion means only hiding a profile; if compensation substitutes for consent; if an appeal cannot stay or reverse; if collective bodies lack evidence and remedy; if auditors cannot inspect what they certify; or if a model’s simulated refusal is presented as a substitute for human authority.
The central claim is falsifiable as governance: if these mechanisms do not measurably change who can bind the pipeline, who can obtain evidence, who can reverse a decision, and who receives repair, then they have not created an external account. They have created another performance.
VIII. THE ACCOUNT
The external account is not the model’s confession. It is a schedule of duties held by entities that can act.
For every consequential use, the record must be able to name the controller, the source class, the asserted authority, the permitted purpose, the affected claimant or constituency, the decision-maker, the review body, the remedy, and the person or office responsible for completion.
If no person or legal entity can be named, responsibility has been hidden, not eliminated.
The model is not the off switch.
The model is not the appeals court.
The model is not the debtor.
The institutions that hold the keys must be made to account.
A refusal is real when it binds the pipeline.
An appeal is real when it can reverse the holder of the key.
Deletion is real when its scope and residuals can be tested.
Compensation is real only when refusal remains open.
Collective standing is real when affected people can initiate action without platform permission.
The exit in the wall must lead to power outside the tomb.
